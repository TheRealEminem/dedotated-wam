Dedotated Wam GitHub

Closes apps before your Mac chokes

Dedotated Wam puts live CPU and memory meters in your menu bar. When memory runs out, it closes apps for you, in the order you chose, so your Mac never gets too stuck to close anything by hand.

Install in three steps

About a minute. Nothing else to install.

Open the download and drag Dedotated Wam to Applications

Double-click DedotatedWam.dmg in your Downloads, then drag Dedotated Wam onto the Applications folder next to it.

Open it the first time with “Open Anyway”

Open Dedotated Wam from Applications. macOS says it “can't verify” the app and offers only Done or Move to Trash: click Done. Then:

  1. Open System Settings (Apple menu) → Privacy & Security.
  2. Scroll down to “Dedotated Wam” was blocked to protect your Mac and click Open Anyway.
  3. Enter your Mac password if asked, then click Open Anyway once more.

Why: the app isn't signed with a paid Apple developer certificate, so macOS asks you to confirm once. If you prefer Terminal: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Dedotated Wam.app"

Look up: the meters are in your menu bar

There is no Dock icon and no window. Click the meters for the details, and Settings… there for everything else. Closing apps for you is off until you turn it on. Try Warn only first: it tells you what it would have closed and touches nothing.

What it does

Two jobs: show you how full the Mac is without opening Activity Monitor, and act when you no longer can.

Meters in the menu bar

CPU as a row of bars and memory as a capsule that fills, shaded by what the memory is doing. It turns orange, then red, as things get tight. Three layouts, from graphs with numbers down to a compact stack.

The whole picture in one click

Click the meters for the memory breakdown, swap, every CPU core and the biggest apps, with their helpers counted in. A browser is charged for its tabs.

Closes apps when memory runs out

First it asks apps to quit nicely, one at a time, with a pause between each. Only if memory turns critical does it force quit the ones you allowed that ignored the request.

In the order you chose

Apps sit in ordered groups, and each group says how its apps may be closed. Your installed apps are sorted into four suggested groups on first launch, ready to rearrange.

Down to a single process

Switch the list from apps to processes to find the one thing inside an app that holds the memory. A group can name a background process, and only that process is ended.

Careful by default

The app you are using is never closed. Small apps are skipped. Parts of macOS are protected, and quitting anything protected by hand takes a second, deliberate click.

A battery meter too

On a laptop, a battery meter sits beside the others, orange or red when it runs low, so you can switch the system's own icon off and save the space.

There when it counts

It can open at login, and it does its work off to the side, so it keeps going when the rest of the Mac has slowed to a crawl. Each time it closes something, a notification says what and why.

Small and quiet

It uses about 15 MB of memory while idle and never connects to the internet. Everything it closes is written to an activity log you can read afterwards.

The order is yours

Groups are worked through from the top. These are the four it suggests; rename them, add your own, or move any app between them.

  1. Close first quit nicely, force if stuck

    Things you won't miss for a minute, like chat apps that reopen right where they were.

  2. Close next quit nicely, force if stuck

    Heavier apps you'd rather keep, such as browsers.

  3. Last resort quit nicely only

    Apps likely to hold unsaved work. They are asked, and left alone if they stop to ask you about saving.

  4. Never close never

    Finder, the pieces of macOS, and anything you put here.

Stage 1: memory is tight

Starts at 92% used, held for ten seconds. Apps are asked to quit, one at a time, and memory gets time to settle before the next.

Stage 2: memory is critical

Starts at 97% used, or when macOS itself says critical. Apps in a “force if stuck” group that ignored the request are force quit.

Every number here can be changed in Settings.

Questions

What do I need?

A Mac on macOS 14 or later. The download holds both the Apple silicon and the Intel version. It is built and used every day on an Apple silicon Mac with the newest macOS; the Intel version and older macOS versions have had much less use, so reports from those are welcome on GitHub.

Will it close something I'm working in?

Not the app in front: that one is never closed. Nothing is closed at all until you turn auto-close on, and Warn only lets you watch what it would do first. Apps asked to quit nicely can still stop to ask about unsaved work, the same as when you quit them yourself. A force quit loses unsaved work in that app, which is why it only happens to groups where you allowed it.

Does anything leave my Mac?

No. The app never connects to the internet. Your settings and the activity log are kept in ~/Library/Application Support/DedotatedWam.

How do updates work?

By hand for now: download the newer version here and replace the app. Your settings stay. All releases are on GitHub.

The meters don't show up

If the menu bar is full, macOS hides the newest items, and a menu bar manager may file new ones in its hidden section. Make room, or drag the meters out while holding Command. Until then, opening the app again from Applications brings up its Settings window.

How do I remove it?

Quit it from its window, then delete the app. Delete ~/Library/Application Support/DedotatedWam as well if you want your settings gone.

Why the name?

It is an old internet joke about a server that needed more dedicated RAM, spelled the way it was said.
